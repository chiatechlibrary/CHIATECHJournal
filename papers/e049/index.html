Abstract

Background: The archived 2016 project designed, simulated and implemented a 24 V-to-19 V voltage-mode PWM buck converter at 30 kHz using a 251 µH inductor, 15 µF output capacitor, 10 mΩ capacitor ESR and 2 mΩ inductor DCR. Its control design selected a 6 kHz crossover target, 60° desired phase margin and a Type-III compensator through a K-factor procedure. Objective: This paper provides a theory-centred re-analysis of the same converter, preserving the source record while reconstructing its switch-state equations, state-space averaged model, small-signal duty-to-output dynamics, CCM/DCM boundary, frequency landmarks and Type-III phase budget. It also audits mathematical inconsistencies that become visible when the archived equations are recomputed. Methods: Ideal CCM switching equations were averaged and linearized around the documented operating point. The rated 3.5 A condition was treated as the principal load case (R = 19/3.5 = 5.43 Ω), while the independently documented 5 Ω schematic load was retained as a separate source branch. Bode response, phase budget and K-factor sensitivity were computed analytically. Results: The ideal duty ratio is 0.7917, Ton = 26.39 µs and Toff = 6.94 µs. The formula-consistent inductor ripple is 0.526 A p-p, giving a CCM boundary current of about 0.263 A and Rcrit ≈ 72.3 Ω. The LC double-pole frequency is 2.594 kHz and the ESR zero is approximately 1.06 MHz. At the archived 6 kHz crossover, the ideal power-stage phase is approximately −158.2°. A 60° phase-margin target therefore requires about +38.2° compensator phase at crossover; when the Type-III integrator’s −90° baseline is included, the two lead sections must supply about 128.2° of lead, corresponding to K ≈ 18.9. The archived 150° boost gives K ≈ 57.7 and is therefore conservative but highly sensitive. The historical report also contains two explicit consistency issues: 3.5 A at 19 V implies 5.43 Ω rather than 5 Ω, and the displayed ripple equation uses Vout while its numerical substitution uses 24 V. Conclusion: The converter is theoretically coherent at the topology and loop-design level, but a modern publication benefits from separating the PWM modulator gain from LC-plant phase, distinguishing parallel source load cases and documenting arithmetic substitutions. The resulting framework is a stronger theoretical basis for simulation, frequency-response verification and future hardware replication.

Keywords: buck converter; state-space averaging; small-signal model; voltage-mode control; Type-III compensation; K-factor; CCM; Bode plot; phase margin; power electronics
