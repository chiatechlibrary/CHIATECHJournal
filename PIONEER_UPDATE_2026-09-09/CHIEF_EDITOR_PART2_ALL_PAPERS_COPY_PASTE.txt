ABSTRACT: Background: Prompt engineering has evolved from ad hoc wording tricks into a broader discipline for specifying tasks, supplying evidence, orchestrating tools and evaluating model behavior. Objective: This article critically synthesizes prompt-engineering research and proposes a reliability-oriented architecture for human–LLM interaction that integrates instruction design, grounding, tool governance, security and verification. ABSTRACT: Background: Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) can support robotic control, navigation and manipulation, but opaque policies create obstacles for debugging, operator trust and safety assurance. Objective: This article is a critical review and deployment framework for explainable reinforcement learning (XRL) in robotics. Methods: The analysis synthesizes XRL taxonomies, explainable-AI concepts and safe-learning literature, organizing methods by stakeholder question, temporal scope and explanation mechanism. Results: Explanations can target state features, action choice, reward structure, trajectories, counterfactual alternatives or policy summaries. Post-hoc attribution, surrogate models, trajectory examples and natural-language summaries can make behavior more inspectable, but explanation quality is multidimensional. Safe robotic deployment requires explainability and control assurance to be designed together.

Conclusion: The most effective rural-energy strategy is not technology deployment in isolation but an integrated productive-use system that begins with agricultural load and resource assessment, sizes hybrid power for reliability, couples energy finance with income-generating equipment, builds local maintenance capacity, governs water and data responsibly, and verifies outcomes such as uptime, crop loss reduction, income, energy cost and emissions. The proposed READF-N framework provides a practical pathway for engineering, financing and governing renewable energy as productive agricultural infrastructure in Nigeria.

ABSTRACT: Micro, small and medium enterprises are central to Nigerian livelihoods and production, yet policy discussion often treats finance, technology, management capability, markets and infrastructure as separate problems. Published finance-gap estimates are reported with their study dates and definitions and should not be treated as interchangeable. SCALE is a testable management and policy architecture whose modules should be adapted by sector, firm maturity and location, then evaluated against explicit counterfactual and cost assumptions.
